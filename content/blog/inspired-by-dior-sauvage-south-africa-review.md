## What Dior Sauvage actually smells like

The original is a masterclass in modern masculine fragrance design. Three things define it:

- **The opening:** sharp, bergamot-citrus brightness with a peppery edge
- **The heart:** a clean, almost mineral lavender-amber that became its signature
- **The dry-down:** the famous ambroxan — that warm, slightly salty, "expensive-skin" smell that defines half the fragrances released in the last decade

It's confident, modern, polished. It works in a boardroom, on a date, at a wedding.

## How our Inspired by Dior Sauvage compares

We rebuilt the same scent architecture in a 20%+ oil-based formula. Here's the honest comparison:

### Opening
The original hits with bright, almost effervescent bergamot. Our version is *very* close — slightly softer in the first ten seconds because oil-based perfumes don't throw the same alcoholic burst. Within a minute, you can't tell them apart.

### Heart
This is where our oil base actually performs *better*. The lavender-amber heart is what people remember about Sauvage, and oil holds these notes far longer than the original alcohol formula. By hour three, the original has faded noticeably. Ours hasn't moved.

### Dry-down
Both finish on that warm ambroxan-amber base. Skin-close, magnetic, the kind of dry-down that gets compliments at close range. Ours sits slightly closer to the skin than the original's projection — which is actually what most South African wearers prefer in a country where office aircon and taxi rides exist.
